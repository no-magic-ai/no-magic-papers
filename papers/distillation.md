## TL;DR

Distillation trains a small model to match the temperature-softened class probabilities of a large model or ensemble, usually mixed with the true labels. The softened probabilities carry how the large model ranks wrong answers, which one-hot labels throw away.

## Problem

Large models and ensembles generalize well but are expensive to deploy. A small model trained only on hard labels learns less than the large model knows. The paper asks how to transfer that knowledge, including how the large model generalizes, into a model cheap enough to deploy. (The arXiv record lists the paper as a NIPS 2014 Deep Learning Workshop contribution.)

## Contribution

The paper generalizes earlier logit matching into distillation. It trains the small model on the large model's softmax outputs at a raised temperature, and shows that logit matching is a special case in the high-temperature limit (§2.1). It also proposes ensembles of specialist models for very large datasets.

## Method summary

- Softmax with temperature (§2, Eq. 1): q_i = exp(z_i/T) / Σ_j exp(z_j/T). A larger T gives a softer distribution.
- Train the distilled model on the cumbersome model's soft targets at the same high T, plus a cross-entropy on the correct labels at T = 1, with "a considerably lower weight" on the hard-label term. After training, the distilled model uses T = 1.
- The gradient of the soft-target cross-entropy with respect to a distilled logit is (q_i − p_i)/T (§2.1, Eq. 2). Because soft-target gradients scale as 1/T², they are multiplied by T² when hard and soft targets are combined, so that their relative contribution stays roughly unchanged as T varies.

## Key results

On MNIST, a large net with two hidden layers of 1200 units made 67 test errors. A net with two hidden layers of 800 units trained without regularization made 146, and the same small net trained to also match the large net's soft targets at T = 20 made 74. A distilled model that saw no 3s during transfer got 98.6% of test 3s right once its bias for that class was adjusted. On speech recognition, one distilled model reached a test frame accuracy of 60.8% and a word error rate of 10.7%, against 58.9% and 10.9% for the baseline and 61.1% and 10.7% for the 10-model ensemble it was distilled from. With only 3% of the speech training data, soft targets gave 57.0% test frame accuracy, against 44.5% for hard targets.

## Relation to existing work

Distillation builds on model compression by Buciluǎ, Caruana and Niculescu-Mizil (2006), whose matching of logits the paper shows to be a special case. It is a different route to cheaper inference from quantization, which keeps the model and lowers its numerical precision. Later work applies it to language models. DeepSeek-R1's "distillation" is supervised fine-tuning on responses generated by a stronger model, not soft-target matching.

## Implementation notes

`no-magic/02-alignment/microdistill.py` is a toy adaptation. It draws 240 training points and 96 separately drawn held-out points from three 2-D Gaussian clusters centered at (−1, 0), (1, 0) and (0, 1.25), with standard deviation 0.35. A ReLU MLP teacher, 2 → 16 → 3 with biases (99 parameters), is trained for 400 full-batch SGD epochs on hard-label cross-entropy and then frozen. A separately initialized 2 → 4 → 3 student (27 parameters) then trains for 400 epochs on the batch mean of 0.9 · T² · KL(p_T ‖ q_T) + 0.1 · CE(y, q_1) with T = 2. The KL form differs from the paper's soft-target cross-entropy only by the teacher's entropy, so the student gradients are identical. The per-example logit gradient is α T (q_T − p_T) + (1 − α)(q_1 − onehot(y)). The batch mean contributes 1/B, where B is the batch size, not the paper's class count N. Both models predict at T = 1.

T = 2 and α = 0.9 are toy choices, not values from the paper, and none of the MNIST, speech, ensemble or specialist experiments is reproduced. The teacher's weight digest must be unchanged after transfer. The program's acceptance criteria are checked on training data only: the teacher's cross-entropy falls by at least 5% and its accuracy reaches at least 80%, and the student's mixed objective and soft-target KL each fall by at least 5%. Held-out accuracy and agreement are reported, not required to improve. Because teacher and student differ in capacity and no hard-label-only student is trained, the program does not measure a causal benefit of distillation.
